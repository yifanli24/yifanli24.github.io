\publication{\textbf{Li, Y.}, \& Lin, M. (2026). A plateau in western US surface ozone: Combined effects of Asian emissions, stratospheric influence, and climate warming.}

\researchsummary{Despite continued reductions in domestic precursor emissions, surface ozone over the Intermountain West (IMW) has plateaued over the last decade. Understanding the processes limiting further ozone reductions is particularly important for air quality management in this region. We combine long-term ozone and meteorological observations with Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Laboratory chemistry-climate model simulations to investigate spring and summer ozone trends and their drivers. Springtime ozone increased before 2012 and subsequently declined, followed by a coherent rebound beginning in 2023, with increases of approximately 3--6~ppbv that year. Increasing emissions from India and Southeast Asia may partly offset Chinese emission reductions, while enhanced stratospheric influence contributed to this rebound. Springtime high-ozone events became more frequent during 2023--2026 relative to 2020--2022. Summer ozone over the IMW changed little during 2009--2023 (+0.02~ppb~yr$^{-1}$). Rising surface temperatures associated with climate warming favor enhanced photochemical ozone production, counteracting the benefits of domestic precursor emission reductions, while wildfires may further contribute to episodic summertime ozone enhancements. These findings highlight how changing Asian emissions, stratospheric influence, and climate warming can sustain elevated WUS ozone and complicate further air quality improvements.}

\publication{\textbf{Li, Y.}, \& Cao, J. (2026). Diagnosing the increase in East Asian summer monsoon precipitation under a low-emissions scenario from an energy perspective.}

\researchsummary{Using multiple climate model simulations together with moisture budget and moist static energy budget analyses, we diagnose the increase in East Asian summer precipitation under the SSP1-2.6 scenario relative to other scenarios. Our results suggest that reduced anthropogenic aerosols over East Asia increase surface solar radiation and evaporation, thereby enhancing convective activity and large-scale upward moisture transport, intensifying the East Asian summer monsoon circulation, and increasing rainfall.}

\publication{\textbf{Li, Y.}, \& Shen, X. (2026). Using causal inference to quantify the roles of tropospheric wave sources and stratospheric preconditioning in sudden stratospheric warmings.}

\researchsummary{Apply a physically based causal inference framework to quantify the relative roles of tropospheric wave sources and stratospheric preconditioning in influencing the occurrence of sudden stratospheric warmings (SSWs). Our results indicate that both factors play essential roles in the occurrence of SSWs. Approximately one-third of the effect of tropospheric wave sources and one-half of the effect of stratospheric preconditioning on stratospheric polar vortex variability are mediated by stratospheric wave-activity fluxes.}
